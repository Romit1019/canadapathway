Express Entry Draw #436 Results: 1000 Invited at CRS 523

Express Entry Draw #436: Canada Issues 1,000 Invitations to Canadian Experience Class Candidates

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #436 on August 18, 2026, issuing 1,000 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates in the Canadian Experience Class (CEC). The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 523, continuing Canada's targeted approach to selecting skilled workers who already have boots on the ground in Canada.

Draw Summary: What Happened

Draw #436 was a program-specific round, meaning only candidates managed under the Canadian Experience Class stream were eligible for consideration — not all-program Express Entry candidates. IRCC issued exactly 1,000 ITAs, a standard volume for CEC-specific draws, signalling a steady and deliberate pace of selection for this particular immigration pathway.

A CRS cutoff of 523 is a competitive but attainable threshold for strong CEC candidates. To reach this score without a provincial nomination — which adds 600 points — applicants typically need a combination of high official language test results, at least one year of skilled Canadian work experience, and in many cases, a Canadian post-secondary credential or advanced education obtained abroad.

Candidates who met or exceeded the 523-point threshold and had a complete profile in the Express Entry pool as of the draw date will receive an ITA, giving them 60 days to submit a full permanent residence application.

What a 523 CRS Cutoff Means for Applicants in the Pool

A cutoff of 523 in a CEC-specific draw reflects the competitive nature of this stream. The CEC is exclusively for individuals who have gained at least one year of skilled work experience in Canada under National Occupational Classification (NOC) TEER categories 0, 1, 2, or 3 within the past three years. Because CEC candidates are already embedded in the Canadian labour market, their profiles tend to be highly competitive.

For context, reaching 523 without a provincial nomination typically requires candidates to demonstrate strong performance across multiple CRS factors. A candidate with a master's degree, CLB 9 scores in English, and one year of Canadian NOC TEER 1 experience, for example, would generally fall in the 520–540 range depending on age and other profile details. This means the draw rewarded well-rounded applicants — not just those with extreme scores in a single category.

Candidates currently sitting in the Express Entry pool with CRS scores between 500 and 522 remain in the pool and eligible for future draws, but should treat this result as a clear signal that proactive score improvement is necessary.

Trend Context: How Draw #436 Compares to Recent CEC Rounds

CEC-specific draws have historically produced CRS cutoffs in the 490–540 range, though scores have fluctuated based on pool composition, IRCC's annual immigration targets, and the volume of invitations issued per round. A cutoff of 523 is solidly mid-range for this stream — higher than draws seen during periods of increased invitation volumes, but not at the elevated peaks observed when IRCC temporarily paused CEC draws and then resumed with a backlog of candidates.

The 1,000-ITA volume is consistent with IRCC's measured approach to CEC draws in recent rounds. Larger invitation volumes tend to push cutoffs down, while smaller, more targeted draws keep cutoffs elevated. The current combination of 1,000 ITAs and a 523 cutoff suggests the pool of high-scoring CEC candidates is healthy but not overwhelming — meaning scores are unlikely to drop dramatically in the near term without a significant increase in draw frequency or volume.

Who Specifically Benefits From Draw #436

The candidates who received ITAs in this draw are largely those who have been strategically building their profiles over time. Specifically, this draw benefits:

  • Workers with Canadian post-secondary credentials who have transitioned into skilled employment and accumulated the required work experience
  • International graduates from Canadian colleges and universities holding Post-Graduation Work Permits (PGWPs) who have moved into NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 roles
  • Candidates with CLB 9 or higher in English or French, whose strong language scores contribute significantly to their overall CRS ranking
  • Applicants who completed a second official language test, picking up additional CRS points that pushed their scores above the cutoff threshold
  • Workers in high-demand sectors such as technology, healthcare, engineering, and financial services, where NOC TEER 0 and 1 classifications are common

What Applicants Just Below 523 Should Do Right Now

If your CRS score falls between 500 and 522, this draw confirms you are close — but close is not enough until you receive an ITA. Here are the most effective steps to close the gap:

  • Retake your language test. Moving from CLB 9 to CLB 10 in even one skill area (reading, writing, speaking, or listening) can add meaningful points to your CRS score. Book an IELTS General Training or CELPIP exam and prepare thoroughly.
