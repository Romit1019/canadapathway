Express Entry Draw #432 Results: 3000 Invited at CRS 516

Express Entry Draw #432: Canada Issues 3,000 Invitations to Canadian Experience Class Candidates

Draw Date: August 5, 2026  |  Draw Type: Canadian Experience Class (CEC)  |  Invitations Issued: 3,000  |  CRS Cutoff Score: 516

Draw Summary: What Happened

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #432 on August 5, 2026, issuing 3,000 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates in the Canadian Experience Class (CEC) stream. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 516, meaning any CEC-eligible candidate who held a profile with a score at or above that threshold received an ITA for permanent residence.

This was a category-specific draw, meaning it was entirely restricted to candidates who have accumulated eligible Canadian work experience. Candidates in the Express Entry pool under the Federal Skilled Worker (FSW) or Federal Skilled Trades (FST) programs were not considered in this round, regardless of how high their CRS score may be.

What a CRS Cutoff of 516 Means for Applicants

A cutoff of 516 is a meaningful benchmark for the CEC pool. For context, CEC-specific draws have historically carried lower cutoffs than all-program draws, given that the CEC pool is smaller and more defined. A score of 516 reflects a candidate who likely brings a combination of strong Canadian work experience, solid language scores, and a post-secondary credential — but does not necessarily require an additional provincial nomination to be competitive.

If your CRS score sits at or above 516 and you are CEC-eligible, this draw confirms that you are well within the competitive range. If you have not yet submitted your Express Entry profile, doing so immediately is strongly advised. CRS cutoffs are not guaranteed to remain consistent between draws — they can shift up or down depending on how many candidates are in the pool, how many ITAs are issued, and whether IRCC runs multiple draws in quick succession.

It is also worth noting that once you receive an ITA, you have 60 days to submit a complete permanent residence application. Missing that window means the invitation expires and you must wait to be selected in a future draw.

Trend Context: How Does This Compare to Recent CEC Draws?

Draw #432 fits within a broader pattern of IRCC conducting regular CEC-specific draws alongside all-program and other category-based rounds. CEC draws have typically seen cutoff scores ranging from the low 490s to the mid-520s depending on pool conditions and the volume of ITAs issued.

A cutoff of 516 with 3,000 invitations indicates a moderately competitive draw. When IRCC issues a higher number of ITAs, cutoffs tend to drop as more candidates are reached further down the score rankings. Conversely, when fewer invitations are issued, the cutoff rises. The 3,000-ITA volume in this draw suggests IRCC is maintaining a steady pace of CEC admissions without significantly expanding or contracting the pool in a single round.

Candidates tracking CEC draw trends should watch whether upcoming draws hold steady near 516 or begin to drift — either direction offers useful signals about how quickly the CEC pool is being drawn down.

Who Specifically Benefits From Draw #432

This draw directly benefits a specific group of Express Entry candidates:

  • Workers with eligible Canadian work experience — at least one year of full-time (or equivalent part-time) skilled work in Canada within the last three years, in a NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation.
  • Candidates with a CRS score of 516 or higher who held an active Express Entry profile on August 5, 2026.
  • Temporary foreign workers and international graduates who transitioned into skilled employment in Canada and have been building their CRS score through Canadian experience, language proficiency, and education.

If you fall into this group and had a valid profile active on the draw date, check your IRCC account — your ITA may already be waiting.

Just Below the Cutoff? Here Is What You Should Do Now

If your CRS score is in the 505 to 515 range, you were not selected in this round — but you are not out of options. There are concrete steps you can take to close the gap:

  • Retake your language test. Improving your IELTS or CELPIP score, even by one band in one category, can add meaningful CRS points. CLB 9 and CLB 10 benchmarks unlock significant score increases.
  • Ensure all experience and education credentials are accurately reflected in your profile. Errors or missing entries can cost you points you have legitimately earned.
  • Explore provincial nominee programs (PNPs). A provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points and guarantees selection in a future all-program draw. Many provinces actively recruit CEC-eligible candidates.
  • Keep your profile active and updated. Expired job offers, outdated language scores, or lapsed profiles can result in you missing a draw you would otherwise qualify for.
  • Monitor draw frequency. If IRCC continues to run CEC draws at a steady pace, the cutoff may dip closer to your score range in coming rounds.
