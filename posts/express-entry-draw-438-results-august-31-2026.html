Express Entry Draw #438 Results: 562 Invited at CRS 697

Express Entry Draw #438: Canada Issues 562 Invitations to Provincial Nominees

Draw Date: August 31, 2026  |  Draw Type: Provincial Nominee Program (PNP)  |  Invitations Issued: 562  |  CRS Cutoff: 697

Draw Summary: What Happened

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #438 on August 31, 2026, issuing 562 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates with a valid provincial nomination. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 697.

This was a Provincial Nominee Program-specific draw, one of the dedicated round types that IRCC uses to target candidates who have already secured a nomination through one of Canada's participating provincial or territorial immigration streams. Every single invitation issued in this round went to a candidate holding a valid provincial nomination — no general pool candidates were selected.

The 562 invitations issued represent a moderate-sized PNP draw, consistent with the operational cadence IRCC has maintained for nomination-specific rounds throughout 2026.

What the 697 CRS Cutoff Actually Means

At first glance, a CRS cutoff of 697 may appear extraordinarily high — and it is, by general pool standards. However, this number is not a meaningful competitive benchmark for candidates without a provincial nomination.

Here is why: when a provincial or territorial government nominates a candidate through an Express Entry-aligned stream, IRCC automatically awards that candidate an additional 600 CRS points. This bonus is added directly on top of whatever score a candidate has earned through their human capital factors — age, education, language proficiency, work experience, and adaptability.

In practical terms, a candidate who scores 95 points based on their own profile alone would see their CRS jump to 695 after receiving a provincial nomination. A candidate with a base score of 100 would sit at 700. This means that virtually any candidate holding a valid nomination would comfortably clear the 697 threshold, regardless of how strong or modest their underlying human capital profile is. The cutoff in PNP draws functions largely as an administrative floor rather than a competitive hurdle.

How This Draw Compares to Recent PNP Rounds

Canada has been running PNP-specific Express Entry draws on a regular basis in 2026, alternating them with Canadian Experience Class rounds, French-language proficiency draws, and broader all-program draws. The 562 invitations issued in Draw #438 fall within the typical range observed for PNP rounds this year, which have generally varied between approximately 500 and 800 invitations per round.

The 697 cutoff score is also consistent with what has been observed across recent PNP draws, where cutoffs routinely fall in the 690 to 730 range — reflecting the reality that the 600-point nomination bonus compresses all nominated candidates into a narrow and elevated scoring band. Differences in the cutoff from draw to draw are driven primarily by the size of the nominated candidate pool at the time of each draw, rather than by shifts in underlying candidate quality.

Who Benefits From This Draw

Draw #438 delivers direct, immediate benefit to one specific group: Express Entry candidates who hold a valid provincial or territorial nomination and who had been waiting in the pool for an ITA. If you received a nomination from a province such as Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Nova Scotia, or any other participating jurisdiction through an Express Entry-linked stream, this round may have resulted in your invitation to apply for permanent residence.

Candidates who benefit most are those who had strong ties to a specific province — perhaps through existing employment, community connections, or prior study — and who pursued a provincial pathway precisely because their base CRS score alone was unlikely to be competitive in general or program-specific draws. The provincial nomination route exists for exactly this purpose: to give provinces the ability to select workers and graduates who meet local labour market needs, even when those candidates would not otherwise rank highly enough for an ITA.

Eligible nominees from streams such as the Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program (OINP), BC PNP Tech, Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP), and similar aligned programs are among those who would have been considered in this draw.

What to Do If You Just Missed the Cutoff

If you hold a provincial nomination and your CRS score fell just below 697 at the time of this draw, the most important step is to ensure your Express Entry profile is complete, accurate, and actively maintained. CRS cutoffs in PNP draws fluctuate with each round based on pool size, so a score that fell short today may clear the threshold in the next draw.

If you do not yet hold a provincial nomination, now is the time to research which provincial streams align with your occupation, education, and location preferences. Securing a nomination remains one of the most reliable and effective strategies for improving your CRS score and accelerating your path to permanent residence.

Review your profile carefully, update any credentials that may have changed, and ensure your language test results are current and accurately reflected in your application.
