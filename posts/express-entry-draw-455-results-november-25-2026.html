Express Entry Draw #455 Results: 6000 Invited at CRS 401

Express Entry Draw #455: Canada Issues 6,000 Invitations to French-Language Proficient Candidates

Draw Date: November 25, 2026  |  Draw Type: French-Language Proficiency  |  Invitations Issued: 6,000  |  CRS Cutoff: 401

What Happened in Draw #455

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #455 on November 25, 2026, issuing 6,000 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates who meet the French-language proficiency criteria under category-based selection. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 401, meaning every candidate at or above that threshold who qualified for this category was eligible to be selected.

This round falls under the second French-language proficiency draw of the 2026 version series, reflecting Canada's ongoing legislative commitment to supporting the vitality of Francophone communities outside Quebec. Category-based selection draws of this type were introduced under Bill C-19 and have since become a cornerstone of Canada's immigration strategy for attracting French-speaking permanent residents to regions where minority Francophone populations are growing.

What a CRS Cutoff of 401 Means for Candidates in the Pool

A cutoff of 401 is a meaningful signal for French-language applicants currently sitting in the Express Entry pool. In general all-program draws, a score of 401 would typically not be competitive — those rounds have historically demanded scores well above 480 in recent years. However, category-based French-language draws operate on a separate selection logic, pulling only from candidates who meet specific linguistic thresholds, which dramatically narrows the eligible pool and suppresses the cutoff score.

What this means in practical terms: if you have a CRS score of 401 or higher and you meet the French-language proficiency requirements, you should already be reviewing your Express Entry profile to ensure it is complete, accurate, and up to date. A score in the 401–420 range, which would be considered modest in a general draw, is genuinely competitive in this category and could translate directly into an ITA in a future round of this type.

How Draw #455 Compares to Recent French-Language Draws

To understand the significance of this result, it helps to look at the broader trend in French-language category draws throughout 2025 and 2026. These rounds have generally maintained CRS cutoffs in the 375–420 range, reflecting the relatively smaller but steadily growing pool of French-proficient candidates. A cutoff of 401 sits comfortably within that historical range, suggesting the pool is neither dramatically undersupplied nor under pressure from a surge of new high-scoring candidates.

The volume of 6,000 invitations is notably substantial. Earlier French-language draws in this series issued between 2,000 and 4,500 ITAs per round. Issuing 6,000 invitations in a single draw indicates that IRCC is accelerating its pace to meet annual Francophone immigration targets, which are tied to the federal goal of having French-speaking immigrants represent a growing share of total annual admissions outside Quebec.

Who Specifically Benefits From This Draw

To be selected in a French-language proficiency draw, candidates must meet one of the following linguistic combinations recognized by IRCC:

  • A Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) score of 7 or higher in French across all four abilities (reading, writing, listening, speaking), with CLB 5 or lower in English — or no English test results at all
  • Alternatively, candidates who demonstrate strong French proficiency at CLB 7+ in French regardless of English ability may qualify depending on IRCC's current category criteria

Candidates who benefit most from Draw #455 include:

  • International students who studied in Francophone institutions or programs in Canada and have strong TEF Canada or TCF Canada scores
  • Workers in Francophone minority communities outside Quebec, particularly in Ontario, New Brunswick, Manitoba, and Alberta
  • Applicants from France, Belgium, Cameroon, Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, and other French-speaking countries with valid language test results showing CLB 7+ in French
  • Candidates with Canadian work experience or a job offer who already have additional CRS points pushing them above the 401 threshold

What to Do If Your CRS Score Is Just Below 401

If your score currently sits in the 380–400 range, you are not out of options — but you need to act strategically. Here is what you should prioritize right now:

  1. Retake your French language test. Even a marginal improvement in your TEF Canada or TCF Canada scores can add meaningful CRS points and push you above the cutoff in a future draw.
  2. Pursue a provincial nomination. A Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) nomination adds 600 CRS points and virtually guarantees an ITA in any draw. Several provinces actively seek Francophone candidates.
  3. Obtain a qualifying job offer. A valid job offer at NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 adds 50 to 200 CRS points depending on your situation.
  4. Maximize your profile completeness. Ensure your education credentials are assessed by a designated organization, that all work experience entries are accurate, and that your language tests are not expired.
  5. Keep your profile active. Express Entry profiles expire after 12 months
