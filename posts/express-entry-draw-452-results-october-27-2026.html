Express Entry Draw #452 Results: 5000 Invited at CRS 407

Express Entry Draw #452: Canada Issues 5,000 Invitations to French-Language Proficient Candidates

Draw Date: October 27, 2026 | Draw Type: French-Language Proficiency (2026 – Version 2) | Invitations Issued: 5,000 | CRS Cutoff: 407

What Happened in Draw #452

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #452 on October 27, 2026, issuing 5,000 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates with strong French-language proficiency. This was a category-based selection draw under the French-Language Proficiency stream, designated as the second such round of 2026. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 407.

This draw is a clear signal that Canada continues to prioritize Francophone immigration outside of Quebec, actively recruiting bilingual candidates who can contribute to French-speaking communities across the country. With 5,000 invitations issued in a single round, this is one of the more substantial category-based draws conducted under this stream.

What a 407 CRS Cutoff Means for Applicants in the Pool

A cutoff of 407 is notably lower than what candidates typically need to receive an invitation through an all-program draw, where cutoffs have historically ranged from the mid-400s to well above 500 depending on pool composition. For French-language proficient candidates, a score of 407 represents a genuinely accessible threshold.

To put this in perspective, a 407 CRS score can realistically be achieved by candidates who may not have a Canadian job offer or significant Canadian work experience, but who hold a post-secondary credential, have several years of skilled work experience, and demonstrate strong proficiency in both French and English. Bilingual candidates — those who score well on both the TEF Canada or TCF Canada for French and the IELTS or CELPIP for English — receive a significant CRS boost that can push profiles well above this cutoff without requiring exceptional scores in other human capital factors.

If your CRS score sits at or above 407 and your Express Entry profile reflects valid French-language test results, you were eligible to receive an ITA in this round — provided your profile was active and complete at the time of the draw.

How This Draw Compares to Recent French-Language Rounds

French-language proficiency draws have consistently delivered lower CRS cutoffs compared to all-program rounds, making them a strategically important pathway for Francophone candidates. The first French-language proficiency draw of 2026 set the benchmark for this year's series, and Draw #452 reinforces a pattern of competitive but accessible cutoffs within this category.

Historically, French-language draws have seen cutoffs ranging from the low 300s to the mid-400s depending on the number of ITAs issued and the composition of eligible candidates in the pool. A cutoff of 407 with 5,000 invitations indicates a robust and growing pool of French-language proficient candidates, while still keeping the threshold meaningfully below what candidates would need in a general draw. This trend strongly suggests that maintaining an active profile with current French-language scores is one of the most effective strategies available to bilingual candidates in 2026.

Who Benefits Most from This Draw

Draw #452 is particularly advantageous for several specific candidate profiles:

  • Bilingual candidates with moderate overall CRS scores: Applicants who score CLB 7 or higher in French and have solid English proficiency receive a combined-language bonus that can add 50 or more points to their CRS, making a 407 cutoff well within reach even for those without Canadian experience.
  • International graduates with French-language skills: Candidates who completed studies in Canada or abroad and have maintained valid French-language test scores are well-positioned in this category.
  • Skilled workers in Francophone communities: Those with job experience in sectors where French is the primary working language — particularly in provinces like New Brunswick, Ontario, or Manitoba — may also carry provincial ties that complement their federal application.
  • Candidates who have been waiting in the pool: Applicants whose profiles have been active for several months and who have not yet received an ITA through all-program draws should take note: category-based draws remain their strongest opportunity in the current environment.

What Applicants Just Below 407 Should Do Right Now

If your current CRS score falls between 380 and 406, you are within striking distance of the cutoff established in Draw #452. Here is what you should prioritize immediately:

  1. Retake your French-language test: If your TEF Canada or TCF Canada results are approaching expiration or were completed when your proficiency was lower, a new test with improved scores could meaningfully increase your CRS.
  2. Improve your English-language scores: Bilingual candidates benefit from strong scores in both languages. Upgrading your IELTS or CELPIP results to CLB 9 or 10 can add additional points.
  3. Update your Express Entry profile: Ensure your education credentials are assessed, your work experience descriptions are accurate and detailed, and your language test results are current. Outdated or incomplete profiles may disqualify you from consideration.
  4. Consider a provincial nomination: A provincial nominee program (PNP) nomination adds 600 points to your CRS instantly. Several provinces actively target Francophone candidates through dedicated streams.
  5. Monitor upcoming draws closely: French-language draws are being held with increasing regularity. Staying prepared means you can act quickly when the next round is announced.
