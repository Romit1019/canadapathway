Express Entry Draw #433 Results: 5000 Invited at CRS 391

Express Entry Draw #433: Canada Issues 5,000 Invitations to French-Language Proficient Candidates

Draw Date: August 6, 2026  |  Draw Type: French-Language Proficiency  |  ITAs Issued: 5,000  |  CRS Cutoff: 391

What Happened in Draw #433

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #433 on August 6, 2026, issuing 5,000 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates with French-language proficiency. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required was 391, marking this round as one of the most accessible French-language draws held in recent history.

This draw falls under the dedicated French-language proficiency category, which IRCC uses to advance Canada's commitment to supporting Francophone immigration outside of Quebec. By reserving draws specifically for French speakers, the federal government aims to strengthen French-speaking communities in provinces such as Ontario, New Brunswick, Manitoba, and British Columbia, helping to maintain the country's linguistic duality.

With 5,000 invitations issued in a single round, this draw represents a significant volume of opportunities for the Francophone applicant pool — a clear signal that IRCC continues to prioritize French-language immigration as a structural pillar of its Express Entry strategy.

What a CRS Cutoff of 391 Means for Applicants

A CRS cutoff of 391 is notably low by Express Entry standards. For context, all-program draws — which include Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class, and Federal Skilled Trades candidates — have historically cleared at scores well above 500 in competitive periods. Even within French-language draws, cutoffs have at times reached into the low-to-mid 400s.

A threshold of 391 means that French-language proficient candidates do not need a high overall CRS score to receive an invitation. Factors such as a strong job offer, Canadian work experience, or a provincial nomination are not prerequisites at this cutoff level. Candidates with modest educational credentials, limited Canadian experience, or lower language scores in English — but strong results in French — may well find themselves above this threshold and eligible for permanent residence.

Practically speaking, if your CRS score sits at or above 391 and your profile correctly reflects your French-language test results from a recognized exam such as TEF Canada or TCF Canada, you were in a strong position to receive an ITA in this draw. If you did not receive one, ensure your profile is active, your language scores are current, and your Express Entry information is fully up to date for the next round.

How This Draw Compares to Recent French-Language Rounds

Draw #433 is the second French-language proficiency draw of 2026, following the first version earlier in the year. The 391 cutoff represents a meaningful drop compared to earlier French-language rounds, which had seen cutoff scores trend slightly higher as the pool of eligible candidates grew and competition increased.

The volume of 5,000 ITAs also stands out. Many French-language draws have issued between 2,000 and 4,000 invitations, making this round notably generous. The combination of a high invitation count and a lower CRS threshold suggests either an expanding Francophone applicant pool, a policy decision to accelerate French-language admissions targets, or both. For applicants who have been waiting in the pool without receiving an invitation, this trend is an encouraging sign.

Who Benefits Most from This Draw

Draw #433 is particularly advantageous for the following groups:

  • Candidates with strong French but modest English scores: French-language proficiency draws reward TEF Canada and TCF Canada results directly, meaning applicants who score well in French but not in English can still compete effectively.
  • Applicants with CRS scores in the 391–420 range: These candidates are often overlooked in all-program draws but are prime beneficiaries of targeted French-language rounds at this cutoff level.
  • International graduates from French-language institutions: Those who studied in Francophone universities in Canada or abroad and have maintained their French proficiency are well-positioned to benefit.
  • Workers in Francophone communities outside Quebec: Candidates with job offers or work experience in provinces actively seeking French-speaking immigrants may hold additional CRS points that push them comfortably above 391.

If Your Score Is Just Below 391: What to Do Now

If your current CRS score falls between 370 and 390, you are close to the cutoff and there are concrete steps you can take to close the gap before the next French-language draw.

First, review your French-language test scores. If your TEF Canada or TCF Canada results are approaching their two-year validity period, retaking the exam and improving your score — even by one level in a single skill — can meaningfully increase your CRS points. Second, verify that all profile information is accurate and complete, including educational credentials, National Occupational Classification codes, and work experience entries. Errors or omissions can artificially suppress your score. Third, explore provincial nominee program streams that target French-speaking applicants, as a provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points and would place virtually any candidate above the cutoff. Finally, consider whether any additional Canadian work experience or a valid job offer could be added to your profile before the next draw.

Cutoff scores in French-language draws can shift between rounds, and a score of 391 today may be the ceiling in the next draw or it may drop further. Staying prepared maximizes your chances of capturing an invitation when conditions are most favourable.
