Express Entry Draw #443 Results: 2000 Invited at CRS 519

Express Entry Draw #443: Canada Issues 2,000 Invitations to Canadian Experience Class Candidates

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #443 on September 15, 2026, issuing 2,000 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates in the Canadian Experience Class (CEC). The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 519, meaning any CEC candidate sitting at or above this threshold on the date of the draw was eligible to receive an ITA for Canadian permanent residence.

Draw Summary: What Happened on September 15, 2026

Draw #443 was a program-specific round targeting only Canadian Experience Class candidates — those with at least one year of skilled work experience gained inside Canada. Unlike all-program draws, which pull from the entire Express Entry pool regardless of program stream, this CEC-specific round completely excluded Federal Skilled Worker (FSW) and Federal Skilled Trades (FST) candidates, no matter how high their CRS scores may be.

The tie-breaking rule, used to determine which candidates are selected when multiple applicants share the cutoff score, was applied to candidates who submitted their Express Entry profile no later than the tie-breaking date established by IRCC for this round. Candidates who met or exceeded the 519 CRS threshold and had Canadian work experience qualifying them for CEC would have received their ITA notification through their IRCC secure account.

With 2,000 invitations issued, this was a moderately sized CEC draw, consistent with IRCC's ongoing approach of running frequent, targeted rounds to manage application volumes across permanent residence pathways.

What a 519 CRS Cutoff Means for Applicants in the Pool

A cutoff of 519 points is a meaningful benchmark for CEC candidates to understand. The CRS scoring system awards points based on age, education, official language proficiency (English and/or French), Canadian work experience, and adaptability factors such as a valid job offer or a spouse's credentials.

For a single applicant without a spouse or common-law partner, reaching 519 typically requires a strong combination of factors — for example, a candidate in their late 20s to mid-30s with a post-secondary credential, Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) scores of 9 or higher in English, and more than one year of Canadian NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 work experience. Candidates who fall short of this threshold remain in the pool but will need to act strategically to improve their scores before the next applicable draw.

How Draw #443 Compares to Recent CEC Rounds

Historically, CEC-specific draws have carried higher CRS cutoffs than all-program draws, reflecting the relatively stronger profiles of candidates who have already lived and worked in Canada. A cutoff of 519 in this round places it in line with recent CEC draw trends, where scores have generally ranged between 515 and 535 over recent months, depending on the number of invitations issued and the overall size of the eligible CEC pool at the time.

When IRCC issues a higher number of invitations in a single CEC draw, the cutoff score tends to decrease slightly as more candidates from lower CRS bands become eligible. Conversely, smaller draws push the cutoff upward. The 2,000-invitation volume in Draw #443 suggests IRCC is maintaining a measured pace of CEC admissions without significantly expanding or contracting the eligible score range compared to prior rounds.

Who Specifically Benefits From Draw #443

The candidates who directly benefit from this draw are those who:

  • Hold a valid Express Entry profile in the Canadian Experience Class stream
  • Have accumulated at least 12 months of skilled Canadian work experience in a TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation within the past three years
  • Had a CRS score of 519 or above at the time of the draw on September 15, 2026
  • Have not previously received an ITA or have a valid profile that has not expired

These candidates will now have 60 days to submit a complete permanent residence application to IRCC. It is critical to gather all required documents — including language test results, work experience evidence, educational credential assessments, and police certificates — as quickly as possible to meet this deadline.
