Express Entry Draw #431 Results: 507 Invited at CRS 768

Express Entry Draw #431: Canada Issues 507 Invitations to Provincial Nominees

Draw Date: August 4, 2026  |  Draw Type: Provincial Nominee Program (PNP)  |  Invitations Issued: 507  |  CRS Cutoff Score: 768

What Happened in This Draw

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #431 on August 4, 2026, issuing 507 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates holding a valid provincial nomination. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 768 points.

This was a Provincial Nominee Program (PNP)-specific draw, meaning only candidates who had already secured a nomination from a Canadian province or territory were eligible. No all-program or category-based invitations were issued in this round. Candidates in the Express Entry pool without a provincial nomination were not considered, regardless of their base CRS score.

What a CRS Cutoff of 768 Actually Means

A score of 768 in a PNP-specific draw can appear alarmingly high at first glance — and understandably so. However, this number requires important context before drawing any conclusions about your own competitiveness.

When a province or territory nominates a candidate through the Express Entry system, IRCC automatically adds 600 points to that candidate's CRS score. This bonus is applied on top of whatever base score a candidate has earned through factors such as age, education, language proficiency, and work experience.

This means the effective base CRS requirement in Draw #431 was just 168 points (768 minus 600). In practical terms, the overwhelming majority of candidates who held a valid provincial nomination at the time of this draw would have comfortably cleared the cutoff. The 768-point threshold is essentially a formality for nominated candidates, not a competitive barrier.

Critically, if you do not hold a provincial nomination, you should not benchmark your CRS score against this draw. The 768 cutoff is not reflective of what scores are needed to receive an ITA through all-program or category-based draws, which operate under entirely different selection criteria.

How This Draw Compares to Recent PNP Rounds

PNP-specific draws have been a consistent feature of Canada's Express Entry calendar, typically running alongside all-program and category-based rounds. Historically, PNP draw cutoffs cluster in the 720–790 range, driven almost entirely by the 600-point nomination bonus rather than variations in base candidate quality.

The cutoff of 768 in Draw #431 sits within the expected range for PNP-specific rounds, suggesting no dramatic shift in pool composition or IRCC selection behaviour. The invitation volume of 507 is modest, reflecting the targeted and nomination-dependent nature of these draws. PNP draws tend to be smaller than all-program rounds precisely because the eligible pool is limited to those who have already navigated a separate provincial application process.

Taken together, Draw #431 represents a routine but meaningful processing event — clearing a portion of the nominated candidate backlog and moving qualified individuals one step closer to permanent residence.

Who Benefits From This Draw

The 507 candidates who received ITAs in Draw #431 share a specific profile:

  • They hold a valid provincial or territorial nomination issued through an Express Entry-aligned stream
  • Their total CRS score — including the 600-point nomination bonus — was at or above 768 points
  • They were actively ranked in the Express Entry pool on August 4, 2026, with an up-to-date and complete profile

Provinces and territories that operate Express Entry-linked PNP streams include Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, Nova Scotia, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, and several others. Each province nominates candidates based on its own labour market needs, demographic goals, and stream-specific criteria — meaning the pathways to nomination vary significantly by province.

What Applicants Just Below the Cutoff Should Do Now

If you hold a provincial nomination and your CRS score fell just short of 768, there are concrete steps you can take immediately:

  • Verify your profile is complete and current. Outdated or missing information can suppress your CRS score unnecessarily. Ensure language test scores, job offers, and educational credentials are accurately entered.
  • Check your nomination status. Confirm that your provincial nomination is still valid and properly reflected in your Express Entry profile, as nominations do have expiry considerations.
  • Retake language tests if possible. Even modest improvements in IELTS or CELPIP scores can meaningfully boost your base CRS, particularly in the speaking and writing components.
  • Monitor upcoming draws closely. PNP-specific draws recur regularly, and cutoff scores fluctuate. A draw in the coming weeks may clear at a slightly lower threshold depending on pool composition.
  • Contact your provincial immigration office if there are any concerns about your nomination's validity or alignment with your Express Entry profile.

For all other candidates without a nomination, the priority should be pursuing provincial nomination through an eligible stream or improving your base CRS score ahead of future all-program and category-based draws.
