Express Entry Draw #453 Results: 150 Invited at CRS 477

Express Entry Draw #453: Canada Issues 150 Invitations for Transport Occupations

Draw Date: October 28, 2026  |  Draw Type: Transport Occupations (2026-Version 2)  |  Invitations Issued: 150  |  CRS Cutoff: 477

1. What Happened in Draw #453

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #453 on October 28, 2026, issuing 150 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) to candidates in the Transport Occupations category. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 477.

This was a category-based selection draw, meaning IRCC targeted candidates whose work experience falls within designated transport-related occupations under Canada's 2026 National Occupational Classification (NOC) framework. Unlike general rounds that pull from the entire Express Entry pool, this draw was exclusively reserved for qualified transport workers, making it a highly selective and niche round. With only 150 invitations on offer, competition among eligible candidates in this occupational category was intense.

2. What a 477 CRS Cutoff Means for Applicants in the Pool

A cutoff of 477 in a category-based transport draw signals a highly competitive threshold. To put this in perspective, a score of 477 typically reflects a candidate profile that combines several strong factors — often including post-secondary education, several years of skilled work experience, and near-ceiling language scores on tests such as the IELTS or CELPIP.

For candidates currently sitting in the Express Entry pool with transport occupation experience, this cutoff is a clear benchmark. If your CRS score is at or above 477 and your occupation falls within the qualifying Transport Occupations list for 2026, there is a strong possibility you were among those invited — or that you are well-positioned for a future draw in this category. If your score falls below 477, you were not reached in this round, and strategic action is needed before the next draw occurs.

It is also critical to verify your NOC code under the 2026 classification. IRCC has updated occupational groupings, and some roles that may intuitively seem transport-related may not qualify under the precise definition used for this draw type. Confirming your code accuracy is a non-negotiable first step.

3. How Draw #453 Compares to Recent Transport Occupation Draws

Draw #453 is designated as Transport Occupations, 2026-Version 2, indicating that IRCC has already held at least one prior transport-specific draw under the updated 2026 NOC framework this year. The relatively small invitation count of 150 suggests IRCC continues to treat transport draws as targeted, supplementary rounds rather than high-volume pulls.

Historically, transport occupation draws have carried CRS cutoffs that vary based on the size of the eligible candidate pool. A cutoff of 477 in a pool of only 150 invitees suggests the eligible transport pool remains relatively limited in size, or that IRCC is being deliberately conservative with invitation volumes. Candidates who track these draws closely should note that cutoffs in category-based draws do not always drop over time — they fluctuate based on who is actively in the pool on the draw date.

4. Who Specifically Benefits from This Draw

Draw #453 directly benefits candidates who meet all of the following criteria:

  • Have an active Express Entry profile submitted before October 28, 2026
  • Hold work experience in a qualifying Transport Occupation under the 2026 NOC, such as transport truck drivers, air pilots, railway traffic controllers, or marine deck officers, among others
  • Achieved a CRS score of 477 or higher at the time of the draw
  • Meet all other Express Entry eligibility requirements under the Federal Skilled Worker Program, Canadian Experience Class, or Federal Skilled Trades Program

For successful invitees, the next step is submitting a complete permanent residence application within the 60-day ITA window. All supporting documents, including language test results, educational credential assessments, and proof of work experience, must be ready and up to date.

5. What Applicants Just Below 477 Should Do Right Now

If you work in a transport occupation and your CRS score fell just short of 477, do not wait passively for the next draw. Take concrete action now:

  • Retake your language test. Even a one-band improvement across CLB levels can add 15 to 30+ CRS points, potentially pushing you above the cutoff threshold.
  • Pursue a Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) nomination. A valid provincial nomination adds 600 points to your CRS score, virtually guaranteeing an ITA in any future draw. Several provinces actively target transport workers through their own streams.
  • Secure a qualifying job offer. A valid job offer from a Canadian employer in a skilled position can add 50 or 200 points to your profile depending on the NOC skill level.
  • Review your profile for scoring errors. Some candidates underreport points for Canadian education, sibling connections, or spouse language scores. A profile audit can reveal untapped points.
  • Stay ready for the next transport draw. IRCC has shown willingness to run multiple draws per category within a calendar year. Keeping your profile updated and documents current ensures you are not caught unprepared.
