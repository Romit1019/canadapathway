Express Entry Draw #441 Results: 3500 Invited at CRS 475

Express Entry Draw #441: Canada Issues 3,500 ITAs to Healthcare and Social Services Workers

Draw Date: September 4, 2026  |  Draw Type: Healthcare and Social Services Occupations (2026 – Version 3)  |  Invitations Issued: 3,500  |  CRS Cutoff: 475

What Happened in Draw #441

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #441 on September 4, 2026, issuing 3,500 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates working in healthcare and social services occupations. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 475.

This was a category-based selection draw, meaning IRCC did not draw from the general Express Entry pool. Instead, only candidates whose Express Entry profiles identified an eligible occupation under the Healthcare and Social Services Occupations category were considered. This is the third version of this category-based draw type conducted in 2026, signalling Canada's continued and deliberate prioritization of workers who can address critical gaps in the country's healthcare and social services infrastructure.

What a CRS Cutoff of 475 Means for Applicants in the Pool

A cutoff of 475 is considered moderately competitive for a category-based healthcare draw. For context, general all-program draws typically require scores well above 500, meaning this draw opened the door for thousands of healthcare and social services candidates who would not yet be competitive in a general round.

If you are a healthcare or social services worker with a CRS score of 475 or above and your profile was active in the Express Entry pool on September 4, 2026, you should have received an ITA. Upon receiving an invitation, you have 60 days to submit a complete permanent residence application to IRCC. Missing this deadline forfeits your invitation, so it is critical to begin gathering your documentation immediately.

Candidates who received an ITA should prioritize collecting police certificates, updated medical exam results, proof of work experience, educational credential assessments, and language test results before their submission deadline.

How This Draw Compares to Recent Healthcare Rounds

Draw #441 continues a pattern of robust federal investment in healthcare immigration throughout 2026. The two previous healthcare category draws this year — Versions 1 and 2 — similarly targeted this occupational group, and IRCC has maintained a high volume of invitations across all three rounds. A cutoff of 475 in the third healthcare draw of the year suggests that the candidate pool in this category remains sizeable, with enough eligible applicants at or above that score threshold to fill 3,500 invitation slots.

Compared to early 2026 healthcare draws, which saw cutoffs ranging between 460 and 480, the 475 cutoff in Draw #441 falls squarely within the established range, indicating a stable and consistent demand for healthcare professionals through the Express Entry system. The steady invitation volumes of 3,000 to 3,500 per draw reflect IRCC's deliberate strategy to use category-based selection as a primary tool for addressing Canada's health human resources shortage.

Who Specifically Benefits From This Draw

Draw #441 benefits Express Entry candidates whose primary National Occupational Classification (NOC) code falls within the approved Healthcare and Social Services Occupations list. This includes, but is not limited to:

  • Registered nurses and licensed practical nurses
  • Physicians and surgeons
  • Pharmacists and pharmacy technicians
  • Physiotherapists and occupational therapists
  • Medical laboratory technologists
  • Paramedics and emergency medical responders
  • Social workers and social service workers
  • Personal support workers and home care aides with eligible NOC codes
  • Dental hygienists and dental assistants

Candidates must hold an active Express Entry profile in either the Federal Skilled Worker Program (FSWP), Canadian Experience Class (CEC), or Federal Skilled Trades Program (FSTP) to be eligible. Simply having healthcare experience is not sufficient — your NOC code as declared in your profile must align with the eligible occupations list published by IRCC for this category.

What Applicants Just Below 475 Should Do Right Now

If your CRS score falls between 455 and 474, you were not included in this round — but you remain well-positioned for the next healthcare category draw. IRCC has demonstrated a clear commitment to running multiple healthcare draws per year, and the gap between your current score and the cutoff may be closeable before the next round is announced.

Here are concrete steps to take immediately:

  • Retake your language test. Improving your IELTS or CELPIP score by even one band in a single category can add 10 to 30 CRS points depending on your profile.
  • Obtain a provincial nomination. A provincial nominee certificate adds 600 CRS points instantly and virtually guarantees an ITA in the next draw you are eligible for.
  • Pursue a qualifying job offer. A valid job offer from a Canadian employer in an eligible NOC can add 50 or 200 points to your CRS score.
  • Gain additional Canadian work experience. If you are currently working in Canada, additional months of skilled work experience may push your score above future cutoffs.
  • Ensure your profile is accurate and up to date. Outdated information can suppress your score — review every section of your profile carefully.
