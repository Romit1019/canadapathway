Express Entry Draw #442 Results: 576 Invited at CRS 734

Express Entry Draw #442: Canada Issues 576 Invitations to Provincial Nominee Program Candidates

Draw Date: September 14, 2026  |  Draw Type: Provincial Nominee Program (PNP)  |  Invitations Issued: 576  |  CRS Cutoff Score: 734

Draw Summary: What Happened on September 14, 2026

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) held Express Entry Draw #442 on September 14, 2026, issuing 576 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates holding a valid Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) nomination. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 734.

This was a targeted, program-specific draw, meaning that no general pool candidates — regardless of their CRS score — were eligible to receive an invitation in this round. Only applicants who had already secured a provincial or territorial nomination and had that nomination reflected in their Express Entry profile were considered.

What Does a CRS Cutoff of 734 Actually Mean?

At first glance, a CRS cutoff of 734 appears extraordinarily high — and for most candidates sitting in the general Express Entry pool, it would be. However, the figure requires important context to be understood accurately.

When a province or territory nominates a candidate through a stream aligned with Express Entry, IRCC automatically adds 600 points to that candidate's CRS score. This 600-point boost is designed to virtually guarantee that nominated candidates receive an ITA in a subsequent draw.

That means the effective base CRS score required in Draw #442 — the score a candidate needed before receiving their provincial nomination — was approximately 134 points. In practical terms, almost any candidate who received a PNP nomination and updated their Express Entry profile would have cleared the cutoff with considerable ease.

For general pool applicants without a provincial nomination, the 734 cutoff is entirely irrelevant. It does not reflect competitive pressure in the open pool and should not be used as a benchmark for evaluating your own CRS standing.

Trend Context: How Does This Compare to Recent PNP Draws?

PNP-specific draws have become a consistent feature of Canada's Express Entry calendar, typically running alongside broader all-program or category-based rounds. Draw #442 falls within a familiar pattern:

  • Invitation volumes in recent PNP draws have generally ranged from approximately 400 to 700 ITAs per round, making the 576 invitations issued here a mid-range figure consistent with recent activity.
  • CRS cutoffs in PNP draws almost always land in the 700–760 range for the same structural reason — the 600-point addition inflates scores across the board for all nominated candidates.
  • The draw size of 576 suggests a moderate backlog of PNP nominees in the Express Entry pool at the time of the draw, neither signalling a surge in provincial nominations nor a dramatic slowdown.

IRCC has continued to use PNP-specific draws as a tool to process provincial nominees efficiently and separately from the broader candidate pool, ensuring that Canada's regional immigration goals are met without displacing general pool applicants from open draws.

Who Benefits from Draw #442?

Draw #442 is directly relevant to a narrow and specific group of applicants:

  • Candidates who received a provincial or territorial nomination through an Express Entry-aligned PNP stream prior to September 14, 2026, and had updated their profile accordingly.
  • Applicants with lower base CRS scores who pursued a provincial pathway strategically, recognizing that a nomination would catapult their total score well above any realistic cutoff threshold.
  • Skilled workers in provinces experiencing labour shortages in specific occupations targeted by streams in provinces such as Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Nova Scotia, and others participating in Express Entry-aligned PNP streams.

If you received a nomination and your CRS score was updated to reflect the 600-point addition before the draw date, there is a strong likelihood your profile was captured in this round.

What Should Applicants Just Below the Cutoff Do Now?

If you are sitting in the general Express Entry pool without a provincial nomination, this draw does not affect your position. However, if you are actively pursuing a PNP pathway and have not yet secured a nomination, here is what you should focus on:

  • Research provincial streams actively. Many provinces run Expression of Interest (EOI) systems that draw candidates from the federal Express Entry pool based on occupation, education, or regional ties. Identify which streams align with your profile.
  • Strengthen your base CRS score. While the 600-point addition makes the cutoff accessible, provinces still set their own internal score thresholds. A stronger base score improves your competitiveness within provincial EOI pools.
  • Improve your language scores. IELTS or CELPIP scores are among the fastest ways to gain CRS points. Moving from CLB 8 to CLB 9 across all four abilities can add significant points to your profile.
  • Pursue a job offer if eligible. A qualifying Canadian job offer adds either 50 or 200 points to your CRS score and may also open pathways to employer-specific PNP streams.
  • Monitor draw frequency. IRCC typically holds PNP draws every few weeks. Ensuring your profile is complete, accurate, and up to date positions you to benefit from the next round.
