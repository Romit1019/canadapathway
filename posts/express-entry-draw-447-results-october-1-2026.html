Express Entry Draw #447 Results: 3500 Invited at CRS 476

Express Entry Draw #447: Canada Issues 3,500 Invitations to Trades Occupations Candidates

Draw Date: October 1, 2026  |  Draw Type: Trades Occupations (2026-Version 3)  |  Invitations Issued: 3,500  |  CRS Cutoff: 476

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) held its third Trades Occupations-specific Express Entry draw of 2026 on October 1, issuing 3,500 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) to eligible candidates with a Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score of 476 or higher. Draw #447 continues Canada's targeted approach to addressing skilled trades shortages across the country, reinforcing the federal government's commitment to filling critical labour gaps in construction, manufacturing, and industrial sectors.

What Happened in Draw #447

On October 1, 2026, IRCC conducted draw #447 exclusively for candidates in trades-designated occupations under the Federal Skilled Trades Program (FSTP) or those otherwise eligible under the trades-specific category introduced through the category-based selection framework. A total of 3,500 ITAs were issued — a substantial round that signals continued strong demand for tradespeople through the Express Entry system.

The minimum CRS cutoff landed at 476 points. Candidates who met or exceeded this score and held an eligible trades National Occupational Classification (NOC) code received an ITA, granting them 60 days to submit a complete permanent residence application to IRCC.

What a CRS Cutoff of 476 Means for Candidates in the Pool

A cutoff of 476 is a meaningful benchmark for trades-category candidates. Unlike all-program draws — which routinely see cutoffs in the 490s or higher — trades-specific draws consistently produce lower thresholds, reflecting the more targeted and smaller eligible candidate pool. For applicants currently sitting at or above 476 in an eligible trades NOC, this draw confirms that your profile is highly competitive and that similar draws in the coming months represent a real pathway to permanent residence.

For those sitting just below 476, the gap to eligibility is manageable. A score in the 460–475 range is within striking distance, particularly if you take deliberate steps to increase your CRS before the next trades-specific draw is held. Historically, IRCC has conducted trades-category draws multiple times per year, meaning the window of opportunity does not close after a single round.

Trend Context: How Does This Draw Compare?

The 2026 trades-specific draws have demonstrated a consistent pattern. The first two trades draws of the year — Versions 1 and 2 — established a competitive but accessible cutoff range for qualified tradespeople. Draw #447 (Version 3) at 476 CRS with 3,500 ITAs reflects stable demand and a pool of candidates that continues to grow as more skilled tradespeople enter the Express Entry system.

Compared to all-program draws, trades-category cutoffs remain notably lower, which is by design. The category-based selection model is intended to prioritize in-demand workers who may not otherwise accumulate the very high CRS scores typically required in general draws. The 3,500 ITA volume is also significant, suggesting IRCC is actively working through the trades-eligible pool and that additional draws in this category before year-end are likely.

Who Specifically Benefits From Draw #447

Candidates who received ITAs in this draw are those who:

  • Hold a CRS score of 476 or higher at the time of the draw
  • Are eligible under the Federal Skilled Trades Program (FSTP) or the trades category-based selection stream
  • Work in eligible NOC codes, including but not limited to electricians (NOC 72200), plumbers (NOC 72300), welders (NOC 72106), heavy-duty equipment operators (NOC 73400), and industrial mechanics (NOC 72400)
  • Have at least two years of full-time skilled trades work experience within the past five years
  • Meet language requirements (minimum CLB 5 for speaking and listening; CLB 4 for reading and writing)
  • Hold either a valid job offer from a Canadian employer or a certificate of qualification issued by a Canadian provincial or territorial authority

If you meet these criteria and held a score of 476 or above on October 1, 2026, you should have received your ITA. Check your IRCC online account immediately and begin preparing your documentation without delay.

What Applicants Just Below the Cutoff Should Do Now

If your CRS score falls between 460 and 475, you are close — and there are concrete actions you can take before the next trades draw is announced:

  1. Retake your language test. Improving your IELTS or CELPIP score by even one band in a single skill area can add 6–30+ CRS points depending on your current level. This is frequently the fastest and most impactful way to boost your score.
  2. Pursue a provincial nomination. A provincial nomination adds 600 points to your CRS score, making you virtually certain to receive an ITA in any draw type. Many provinces actively recruit skilled tradespeople through their own streams.
  3. Secure a valid job offer. A qualifying Canadian job offer at NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 adds 50–200 points to your CRS, depending on the occupation level.
  4. Update your profile. Ensure your Express Entry profile reflects your most current credentials, employment history, and language scores. Outdated profiles can cost you points you have already earned.
