Express Entry Draw #434 Results: 300 Invited at CRS 470

Express Entry Draw #434: Canada Issues 300 Invitations to Transport Occupation Workers

Draw Date: August 7, 2026  |  Draw Type: Transport Occupations (2026-Version 2)  |  Invitations Issued: 300  |  CRS Cutoff: 470

Draw Summary: What Happened

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #434 on August 7, 2026, issuing 300 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates working in transport occupations under Canada's 2026 NOC framework. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 470, marking this as one of the more selective category-based rounds issued under the transport occupations stream.

This draw is designated as the second transport occupations draw of 2026, labelled "2026-Version 2," indicating that IRCC has continued its targeted approach to filling labour gaps in this sector throughout the year. With only 300 ITAs issued, the pool of successful candidates was deliberately narrow, reinforcing the competitive nature of category-based selection when invitation volumes are kept low.

What a CRS Cutoff of 470 Means for Applicants in the Pool

A cutoff of 470 is meaningfully high for a category-based draw. To put that number in context, general all-program draws in 2025 and 2026 have frequently landed in the 480–510 range, meaning this transport draw is competitive but still provides a pathway for candidates who may not yet rank highly enough to be selected in an all-program round.

For most transport occupation candidates, reaching 470 typically requires a combination of strong language scores, Canadian or foreign work experience, post-secondary education, and ideally a valid job offer or provincial nomination — though neither is strictly required. A candidate with Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) 9 across all four language abilities, a three-year degree, and three or more years of skilled work experience may be positioned at or near this threshold.

Candidates who scored exactly 470 on the tie-breaking date set by IRCC were included in this round. Those who fell even one point below that mark did not receive an invitation, making profile optimization critical for anyone in this occupational category.

Trend Context: How Draw #434 Compares to Recent Transport Draws

Canada's category-based selection system, introduced under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act amendments, has given IRCC the flexibility to target specific labour market needs. Transport occupations have been a recurring category since the program's expansion, reflecting persistent demand across trucking, rail, aviation ground operations, and urban transit sectors.

The 2026-Version 2 designation signals that this is not a one-off event — IRCC has already conducted at least one prior transport draw in 2026. Compared to many category-based draws in other sectors that have issued 500 to 1,000+ invitations, this draw's volume of 300 is notably restrained. This smaller pool may indicate that IRCC is managing inventory carefully or that eligible, high-scoring candidates in this specific occupational category are fewer in number relative to other streams.

A CRS cutoff of 470 for transport workers is consistent with the competitive but accessible range seen in earlier occupation-specific draws, suggesting IRCC is maintaining steady selection standards for this category rather than dramatically raising or lowering the bar.

Who Specifically Benefits from This Draw

Draw #434 targeted candidates whose Express Entry profiles list a primary occupation that falls under Canada's transport occupations category in the 2026 NOC framework. This broadly includes professionals such as:

  • Transport truck drivers and long-haul operators
  • Bus drivers and transit operators in urban and intercity systems
  • Railway traffic controllers and marine traffic regulators
  • Aircraft assemblers and inspectors in transport-adjacent roles
  • Delivery and courier service drivers in eligible NOC classifications
  • Supervisors in motor transport and related ground crews

To have been selected, these candidates must have held an active Express Entry profile accurately reflecting their occupation, scored at or above 470 CRS, and been registered in the pool at the time of the draw. Candidates with Canadian work experience in these fields or those holding provincial nominations would have had a significant scoring advantage.

What Applicants Just Below the Cutoff Should Do Right Now

If your CRS score is in the 450–469 range and you work in a transport occupation, do not become discouraged — category-based draws can repeat, and your score can still improve. Here are concrete steps to take today:

  1. Retake your language test. Even a one-band improvement in a single IELTS or CELPIP component can add 10 to 30 CRS points depending on your current level. This is the fastest way to boost your score meaningfully.
  2. Verify your NOC code. Ensure your Express Entry profile is mapped to the correct 2026 NOC occupation code. An incorrect code could disqualify you from category-based draws entirely, regardless of your score.
  3. Explore provincial nominee programs (PNPs). Several provinces actively recruit transport workers through streams aligned with Express Entry. A provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points — an instant pathway to selection.
  4. Update your work experience entries. If you have recently crossed a threshold (from one year to two years, or two to three years of skilled experience), update your profile immediately, as this directly affects your CRS total.
