Express Entry Draw #450: Canada Issues 5,000 Invitations to French-Language Proficiency Candidates

Draw Date: October 16, 2026  |  Draw Type: French-Language Proficiency  |  Invitations Issued: 5,000  |  CRS Cutoff: 435

What Happened in Draw #450

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #450 on October 16, 2026, issuing 5,000 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates in the French-language proficiency category. This is the second French-language proficiency draw of 2026, making it a significant moment for bilingual candidates who have been patiently waiting in the pool.

The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 435. Candidates at or above this threshold who met the French-language proficiency eligibility criteria received ITAs to apply for Canadian permanent residence. With 5,000 invitations issued in a single round, this draw ranks among the larger targeted draws Canada has conducted, signalling a continued and deliberate effort to attract French-speaking newcomers to support the country's francophone communities outside Quebec.

What a CRS Cutoff of 435 Means for Applicants

A cutoff of 435 is notably accessible by Express Entry standards. In all-program draws, cutoffs frequently exceed 480 or even 500, meaning many skilled candidates with strong profiles are routinely passed over simply because their overall CRS score isn't high enough. For French-language proficiency draws, the dynamics are fundamentally different.

A score of 435 means that bilingual candidates — those with strong French skills but moderate English ability or other profile factors that would otherwise limit their CRS score — have a genuine, realistic pathway to permanent residence. You do not need to be a top-tier applicant across every category to qualify. What matters most in this stream is your French proficiency level. Candidates who hold at least a Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) 7 in French and either CLB 5 or lower in English, or no valid English language test, may qualify for this category.

A score of 435 can realistically be achieved by candidates with a post-secondary diploma or degree, a year or more of skilled work experience, and a strong French-language test result such as a TEF Canada or TCF Canada score placing them at CLB 7 or above. For many applicants in francophone communities or those who studied in French-language institutions, this cutoff is within reach.

Trend Context: How Does This Draw Compare?

Draw #450 is the second French-language proficiency draw of 2026, following the first round earlier in the year. Canada has been steadily increasing both the frequency and volume of French-language draws since the government introduced category-based selection in 2023. These draws directly support Canada's francophone immigration targets, which aim to increase the share of French-speaking newcomers settling outside Quebec to 8.5% by 2026.

Compared to earlier French-language draws that issued between 2,000 and 3,500 invitations, the 5,000 ITAs issued in Draw #450 represent a meaningful increase in volume. The CRS cutoff of 435 is broadly consistent with previous French-language proficiency rounds, which have generally ranged between 420 and 470 depending on pool composition. This stability in the cutoff range is encouraging for candidates who have been building their profiles specifically for this category.

Who Specifically Benefits From Draw #450

Draw #450 delivers the most direct benefit to several groups of candidates:

  • Francophone international students in Canada who completed degrees or diplomas at Canadian institutions and hold strong French test scores but may not yet have extensive work experience pushing their CRS above all-program draw cutoffs.
  • Temporary foreign workers in francophone communities — particularly in provinces like New Brunswick, Ontario, or Manitoba — who speak French as a primary working language and have accumulated Canadian work experience.
  • Candidates from French-speaking countries such as France, Morocco, Senegal, Cameroon, Haiti, and Lebanon who meet the CLB 7 French threshold and have skilled work experience either in Canada or abroad.
  • Bilingual professionals who deliberately chose not to submit an English language test or whose English scores fall below the threshold that would otherwise remove them from French-language category eligibility.

If your CRS score sits between 435 and 460 and you meet the French-language proficiency criteria, Draw #450 would have captured your profile. Candidates who received an ITA should act promptly — you typically have 60 days to submit a complete permanent residence application.

What Applicants Just Below the Cutoff Should Do Now

If your CRS score fell just below 435 in this round, do not be discouraged. Given the trend toward larger and more frequent French-language draws, there is a strong likelihood that future rounds will continue to issue invitations at comparable or even lower cutoff scores. In the meantime, there are concrete steps you can take to strengthen your profile:

  • Upgrade your French test score. Moving from CLB 7 to CLB 9 or higher in French can add significant CRS points and also strengthens your competitiveness within the category.
