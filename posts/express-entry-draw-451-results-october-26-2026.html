Express Entry Draw #451 Results: 792 Invited at CRS 761

Express Entry Draw #451: Canada Issues 792 Invitations to Provincial Nominees

Draw Date: October 26, 2026  |  Draw Type: Provincial Nominee Program (PNP)  |  Invitations Issued: 792  |  CRS Cutoff: 761

What Happened in Draw #451

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #451 on October 26, 2026, issuing 792 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates with a valid provincial nomination in hand. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 761 points.

Because this was a Provincial Nominee Program-specific draw, the high cutoff score is largely a reflection of how the PNP bonus works within Express Entry — not a signal that competition has intensified for general skilled workers. Candidates who receive a provincial nomination through a stream aligned with the federal Express Entry system automatically receive an additional 600 CRS points, which is designed to fast-track them toward a federal Invitation to Apply. Stripping away that 600-point bonus, the effective base CRS score required from a candidate's own human capital factors — age, education, language, and work experience — was approximately 161 points. That is a low threshold by any measure, confirming that virtually all confirmed provincial nominees currently in the Express Entry pool were captured by this draw.

What This CRS Cutoff Actually Means

A cutoff of 761 can look alarming at first glance, but it is important to understand that this number is almost entirely composed of the 600-point nomination bonus. If you do not yet hold a provincial nomination, this cutoff has no practical bearing on your standing in the Express Entry pool. General-category draws, Canadian Experience Class draws, and Federal Skilled Worker draws operate on entirely separate selection criteria and have historically produced cutoffs ranging from the low 400s to the mid-500s.

For candidates who do hold a valid provincial nomination and were not selected in this round, the situation requires closer attention. A base score below 161 points — before the nomination boost — would be unusual, but if your profile contains errors, your nomination has expired, or your Express Entry profile is not properly linked to your provincial nomination, you may have been excluded from the eligible pool. Verifying the accuracy of your profile in the IRCC system should be your immediate next step.

How Draw #451 Compares to Recent PNP Rounds

PNP-specific draws tend to be smaller in volume than all-program draws because they are constrained by the actual number of confirmed nominees sitting in the Express Entry pool at any given time. With only 792 invitations issued, this was a notably compact draw, sitting on the lower end of the range seen in recent PNP rounds. By comparison, larger PNP draws in the past have exceeded 1,000 invitations when a backlog of nominees has accumulated over several weeks between draws.

The relatively small invitation count in Draw #451 suggests that IRCC is running PNP draws frequently enough to prevent a significant buildup of provincial nominees in the pool — a sign of a well-paced processing rhythm rather than a restrictive policy shift. Applicants should not interpret the modest invitation count as a tightening of immigration targets.

Who Benefits Directly from This Draw

The 792 candidates who received ITAs in Draw #451 share a very specific profile: they had already secured a provincial or territorial nomination through an Express Entry-aligned stream and had an active, complete Express Entry profile at the time of the draw. These individuals likely applied through streams such as:

  • Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program (OINP) — Human Capital Priorities Stream
  • British Columbia PNP — Skills Immigration (Express Entry BC)
  • Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP) — Alberta Express Entry Stream
  • Nova Scotia Nominee Program — Labour Market Priorities Stream
  • Other province- and territory-specific Express Entry-aligned streams

With an ITA now in hand, these candidates have 60 days to submit a complete permanent residence application. Missing that deadline means forfeiting the invitation entirely, so prompt action on gathering documents — language test results, police certificates, medical exams, and educational credential assessments — is critical.

What Applicants Just Below the Cutoff Should Do Now

If you hold a provincial nomination and believe you should have been included in this draw but were not, review your Express Entry profile immediately to confirm that your nomination is properly recorded, unexpired, and matched to your federal profile. Contact your provincial nominee program office if you suspect a technical discrepancy.

If you are currently pursuing a provincial nomination but have not yet received one, the most productive steps you can take right now are:

  • Research active PNP streams in provinces that align with your occupation and language profile
  • Improve your base CRS score by retaking language tests, upgrading your educational credential assessment, or gaining additional Canadian work experience
  • Maintain an up-to-date Express Entry profile so you are eligible the moment a nomination comes through

For general-category candidates who are not pursuing a provincial nomination, continue monitoring all-program and category-based draws, which operate independently and select candidates on the basis of human capital scores alone.
