Express Entry Draw #456 Results: 600 Invited at CRS 547

Express Entry Draw #456: Canada Issues 600 Invitations to STEM Occupation Candidates

Draw Date: November 26, 2026  |  Draw Type: STEM Occupations (2026 – Version 3)  |  Invitations Issued: 600  |  CRS Cutoff: 547

1. What Happened in Draw #456

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Express Entry Draw #456 on November 26, 2026, issuing 600 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to candidates in STEM occupations under the category-based selection stream. The minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score required to receive an invitation was 547 — a high threshold that places this round among the more selective STEM draws held in 2026.

This draw is designated as the third STEM-category draw of 2026 (Version 3), reflecting Canada's continued prioritization of science, technology, engineering, and mathematics professionals as part of its long-term economic immigration strategy. STEM category draws target candidates whose NOC codes align with high-demand technical occupations, allowing IRCC to invite them independently of the general all-program pool.

2. What a 547 CRS Cutoff Means for Candidates in the Pool

A cutoff of 547 is a meaningful barrier for the majority of Express Entry candidates. To put this in perspective, scoring 547 or higher typically requires a combination of several strong factors — most candidates reaching this threshold possess:

  • A master's degree or higher, or a completed Canadian post-secondary credential
  • Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) scores of 9 or above in all four language abilities
  • Several years of skilled work experience, ideally including Canadian experience
  • A provincial nomination or valid Canadian job offer (which alone adds 50–600 points depending on type)

Candidates sitting below 547 in a STEM-eligible occupation are not currently competitive in the category-based pool at this cutoff level. Without deliberate score improvements, they risk waiting through multiple draw cycles with no guarantee of an invitation.

3. Trend Context: How Does This Compare to Recent STEM Draws?

Draw #456's cutoff of 547 signals a tightening in STEM-category competitiveness through late 2026. STEM draws have historically produced a wide range of cutoffs depending on pool composition, invitation volumes, and broader immigration targets. A cutoff in the upper 540s suggests the STEM pool has grown more competitive — either because more high-scoring candidates have entered, or because IRCC has deliberately constrained invitation volumes to manage application intake.

The relatively modest allocation of 600 invitations further contributes to the high cutoff. When fewer ITAs are available, only the top-ranked candidates in the pool clear the threshold. Candidates who have been following STEM draws throughout 2026 should take note: if cutoffs are rising and invitation volumes are not increasing proportionally, it becomes increasingly difficult to receive an ITA without a proactive score-boosting strategy.

4. Who Specifically Benefits from Draw #456

The 600 candidates who received invitations in this round are likely to share a specific profile. This draw directly benefits:

  • Senior technology and engineering professionals with five or more years of experience in NOC STEM-eligible categories, whose accumulated points from age, experience, and education push them above 547
  • Provincial nominees in STEM fields — a provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points, virtually guaranteeing an ITA in any category draw
  • Candidates with a valid employer-specific job offer at an appropriate NOC skill level, which adds 50–200 additional points
  • Recent graduates with Canadian credentials who have secured skilled employment in a STEM occupation and benefit from the Canadian education and experience bonuses
  • Bilingual STEM candidates with strong scores in both English and French, as French-language proficiency carries significant additional CRS points
