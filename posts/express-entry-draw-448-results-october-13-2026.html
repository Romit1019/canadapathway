Express Entry Draw #448 Results: 752 Invited at CRS 772

Express Entry Draw #448: Canada Issues 752 Invitations to Provincial Nominees

Draw Date: October 13, 2026  |  Draw Type: Provincial Nominee Program (PNP)  |  Invitations Issued: 752  |  CRS Cutoff: 772

Draw Summary: What Happened

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) conducted Draw #448 on October 13, 2026, issuing 752 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) exclusively to Express Entry candidates who hold a valid provincial nomination. The Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) cutoff score for this round was 772.

This was a targeted Provincial Nominee Program draw, meaning that only candidates who had already secured a nomination from one of Canada's participating provinces or territories were eligible to receive an invitation. No candidates without a provincial nomination — regardless of how strong their education, work experience, language scores, or other human capital factors were — could receive an ITA in this round.

The 752 invitations issued represent a moderate draw size, consistent with the cadence IRCC has maintained for PNP-specific rounds throughout 2026 as it continues to manage permanent residency levels targets under the current Immigration Levels Plan.

What the 772 CRS Cutoff Actually Means

At first glance, a cutoff of 772 may appear extraordinarily high. However, this figure is almost entirely explained by the mechanics of how provincial nominations interact with the Express Entry CRS scoring system.

When a province or territory nominates a candidate through the Express Entry-linked stream, that candidate automatically receives an additional 600 points added directly to their CRS score. This means the true measure of a candidate's underlying human capital profile — their age, education, language ability, and work experience — only needs to reach approximately 172 points to cross the 772 threshold in a PNP draw.

A score of 172 in the core human capital factors is considered quite accessible. For context, a candidate in their late 30s with post-secondary education, mid-range Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) scores in the 7–8 range, and at least one year of skilled work experience would typically meet or exceed this underlying benchmark. This means the practical barrier for provincially nominated candidates in Draw #448 was low — the nomination itself is the decisive factor.

Trend Context: How Draw #448 Compares to Recent PNP Rounds

Draw #448 fits squarely within the established pattern for PNP-specific Express Entry rounds in 2026. Recent comparable draws have seen cutoff scores cluster in the 770–775 range, reflecting the consistent application of the 600-point nomination bonus across a pool of candidates with varied underlying profiles.

The invitation count of 752 is broadly in line with recent PNP rounds, which have generally ranged between 600 and 900 invitations depending on provincial nomination volumes and IRCC's monthly processing targets. This draw does not signal any dramatic policy shift, but it does reaffirm that IRCC continues to run dedicated PNP draws at regular intervals alongside general and category-based rounds — a practice that has become a reliable feature of the Express Entry calendar.

Who Specifically Benefits From This Draw

Draw #448 directly benefits the following candidates:

  • Express Entry-aligned provincial nominees: Candidates who applied through and received a nomination from an Express Entry-linked provincial stream, such as the Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program (OINP), British Columbia PNP Tech stream, Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP), or comparable streams in other provinces.
  • Candidates with modest core CRS scores: Because the underlying human capital requirement is only around 172 points, nominees who might otherwise wait years in the general Express Entry pool can receive an ITA relatively quickly once nominated.
  • Workers in provincially prioritized occupations: Many provincial nominations are issued to candidates in sectors facing regional labour shortages — trades, healthcare, agriculture, and technology — meaning this draw disproportionately rewards individuals filling real gaps in provincial labour markets.

What Applicants Just Below the Cutoff Should Do Now

If you hold a provincial nomination and your CRS score fell just below 772 in this round, your path forward is clear and encouraging. PNP draws occur frequently, and IRCC typically holds multiple rounds each month across different draw types. Candidates with valid nominations who were not reached in Draw #448 remain well-positioned for an upcoming PNP-specific round.

In the meantime, consider taking these concrete steps:

  1. Verify your nomination status: Confirm that your provincial nomination certificate is current and has not expired. An expired or withdrawn nomination removes your 600-point bonus entirely.
  2. Update your Express Entry profile: Ensure all information in your profile is accurate and up to date, including language test scores, education credentials, and job offer details if applicable.
  3. Retake language tests if eligible: If your CLB scores have room for improvement, even a marginal gain in your IELTS or CELPIP results can add meaningful points to your underlying human capital score.
  4. Monitor draw results closely: Sign up for IRCC notifications and follow trusted immigration news sources to stay informed about the next PNP draw date and cutoff trend.

If you do not yet hold a provincial nomination, this draw does not apply to you — but it underscores the significant strategic advantage that securing a provincial nomination provides. Researching provincial streams aligned with your occupation and province of interest should be a top priority in your immigration planning.
